# Where the evaluator and improver can still gain

This analysis recomputes component effects from the audited six-mechanism cross-component test and the newly frozen incumbent-aware transfer banks. The latter labels are now open and are **development evidence only** for designing the next experiment. Source: `analysis/seed-bottleneck-dissection-20260918.json`, generated by `analyze_seed_bottlenecks.py`.

## Separate the four limits

| Limit | Observable test | Evidence | Consequence for the seed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Candidate coverage | Does any generated program pass fifteen final examples? | In the 288-task cross-component test, AST widening raised AST reachability **9 to 23/48**. On the newer 576-task mixed set, total reachability was **492/576**. | Search edits matter where the correct answer is absent. Measure coverage before crediting ranking or sensor edits. |
| Ranking | Among reachable candidates, does the evaluator select a correct one without a query? | On the newer set, **304/576** were correct without a query, leaving **188 reachable misses**. In the cross-component test, the AST replay ranker raised final correctness **145 to 148/288** on the same fixed map. | Train and route a ranker by public task context, and track reachable-but-missed cases separately. |
| Information use | Does a true external output change a wrong decision? | One top-20 query per task on the newer set would raise correctness **304 to 424**, at **576 calls**. There were 120 gains and zero harms in this sample. In the 288-task cross-component test, an AST query raised **148 to 159** at 48 calls. | Keep the output sensor external and budget its calls. One-query-each is a diagnostic upper-cost arm, not the deployed budget. |
| Resource allocation | Which eight of 48 tasks deserve a query? | On the newer twelve banks, the learned allocator selected **349/576** at **96 calls**. An unavailable hindsight choice of eight calls per bank would reach **396**, leaving **47 choices** of allocation regret. The strongest fixed schedule scored **341**. | Improve prediction of *marginal realized query gain* and uncertainty; do not equate lower forecast error with better final choices. |

The cross-component test also shows why a single global edit is unsafe: sharpening the listwise loss everywhere reduced correctness **145 to 139/288**, with losses on transformation and self-edit tasks. AST widening and replay ranking helped the AST branch, while one external query supplied most of its observed final-choice gain. The shadow adaptive AST sensor reached 161 versus fixed top-20 at 159, but that two-choice edge did not clear its transfer gate. The supported integration is therefore a public-context route with an AST search/ranker branch and an externally budgeted output sensor, plus a feedback-trained mixed-task query allocator where its acquisition and inference costs are acceptable.

## What a good evaluator must do

For this benchmark, evaluator quality is a vector, not one score: candidate reachability, top-choice correctness conditional on reachability, false accepts, response to exact output feedback, calibration of predicted query gain, and performance at a fixed total call and compute budget. The exact interpreter supplies external final labels; the evaluator and improver cannot edit it. The visible Unicode block is a powerful mechanism cue in these six synthetic families, so missing or misleading cues require a new transfer test. Final correctness here is agreement on fifteen held-out examples, not universal equivalence.

## What a good improver must do

An improver should select edits that increase **external final correctness per resource cost** on the same frozen base, then transfer that ability to a child improver after new verified feedback. Every edit trial needs a component, parent state, public task context, source and inference costs, reachability/ranking/query effects, harms, gate decision and descendant outcome. A fixed menu is acceptable for a seed test, but it limits the RSI claim. The definitive growth contrast is later-parent child versus initial-parent child on untouched banks, alongside frozen-parent, scrambled, duplicate, strongest fixed and outside-rule controls.

The two fresh cross-family training-rule studies did **not** show that contrast. First, the later child scored 340 versus initial child 342/576 after a catalog tie-break selected ridge-100. Then an incumbent-aware tie correction scored 349 versus 349 on another 576 tasks; its fixed ridge-10 control also scored 349. More historical feedback and a better-looking tie rule did not make a stronger child. This points to the edit space and value-of-information model as larger bottlenecks than tie-breaking between nearly identical ridge penalties.

## Next discriminating integration test

Use one common six-mechanism base and a small **typed edit catalog**: AST candidate widening, AST replay ranker, public-context temperature route, external probe selector, cross-family query allocation, and rollback. Include the harmful global-sharp edit as a negative control. Measure each edit alone, compatible bundles, and leave-one-component-out bundles on the same tasks. For each package, log four effects separately: added reachable tasks, recovered ranking misses, query-induced choice changes, and call/compute cost. Train the improver on balanced verified edit effects, not only successful edits; freeze its parent and child policies before new labels. Require a protected admission gate, then a later-parent-to-better-child result on disjoint mixed and cue-disrupted banks under identical new-feedback and inference budgets. Charge unequal historical acquisition separately.

The seed to carry forward today is the **fixed public-context evaluator route plus AST coverage/ranker branch and a budgeted external output sensor**. The learned cross-family call allocator is a supported scoped component from its earlier predeclared transfer result. It scored 349 versus 341 for the strongest fixed schedule here, but this new comparison had seven positive banks and a **0 to +17** paired bank interval, so this replication alone is inconclusive. It is not a proven self-improving improver. The next experiment should optimize the 47-choice allocation gap while allowing edits that also change reachability and ranking. No current result supports declaring an optimal general RSI seed.
